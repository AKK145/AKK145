Short Biography

I was cooking alongside my mom when I took a bite of bread that was toasted to a crispy, buttery perfection. Inside, a generous layer of melted cheese oozed and stretched out as I pulled the halves apart to dip in the velvety tomato soup. As I took my second bite, my mom abruptly handed over her phone to me, nudging me to speak with my grandma. I quickly wiped my hands and took the phone. As I opened my mouth to speak, nothing but a dejected sigh came out: I couldn’t utter a sentence.

In Tamil, I can ask my parents “What’s for dinner?”, but can’t have a sophisticated discussion on why pineapples should never be on pizza. My inability developed into indifference to Tamil over time as I wasn’t able to communicate my full thoughts through this medium. I got all my useful recipes in English from my mom, so what did I need Tamil for?

Indifference turned to dread when I visited my grandparents in Chennai, India for their 80th birthday two summers ago as I didn’t know how to communicate with anyone. I knew relatives would jab: “Do you speak Tamil? Do you speak Tamil?” Searching for comfort in this new environment, I went into my grandparents’ kitchen to make myself a grilled cheese. As I opened the fridge, my grandmother walked in and tried to communicate in her best English:

“You know make Sambar?” she said. I loved the taste of sambar (lentil soup); its enticing fragrance of coriander, cumin, and fenugreek seeds amalgamating with carrots, potatoes, and drumsticks are tied all together by yellow dal, giving the soup a creamy texture. While I ate sambar a lot at home, I never learned the recipe.

“No,” I said.

“I show you,” she responded excitedly.

As I helped her chop tomatoes and green chilies, I had a palpable sense of curiosity mixed with apprehension. I was worried about speaking in Tamil because I didn’t want to say the wrong words, but I also wanted to share in my grandmother’s cooking. To locate the spices, I drew pictures of ingredients on the margins of the 3-year-old newspaper that lined the open cabinets. Recognizing my drawing, she directed me to the right cabinet and a game of Pictionary ensued as she drew pictures of more ingredients for me to find. I spoke back in broken Tamil and was not met with the expected jabs. At that moment, a bridge formed between generations built not just on language but on the universal language of love and understanding. Through our shared efforts in the kitchen, I was able to step into her shoes, appreciating not only her cooking style and techniques but her empathy and understanding. It was as if the flavors we were creating together were not just a symphony of tastes, but a beautiful melody of connection, binding us in a way words never could.

I have realized that this experience was much more than a meal. Through my grandmother, I’ve learned to unravel the layers that constitute each person’s unique story. By cooking with others, I have come to realize that relatability is not a static condition, but a fluid and malleable quality that can be unearthed through open-hearted engagement. Whether in my advocacy work in the DC Practice & Ethics Advisory Committee or mentoring a new student at my school, I aim to lead with empathy and a curiosity to learn more about those around me.

Cooking reminds me that beneath our apparent differences, there exists a profound interconnectedness that, when acknowledged and embraced, has the power to bridge even the widest of divides. As I embark on my journey to become a better advocate, physician, and chef, I carry with me the lessons I've learned in the kitchen with a strong belief in the power of compassion.

Future Plans

My future plans include pursuing a medical degree with the goal of becoming a physician who not only provides high-quality care but also works to address systemic health disparities. As an advocate for health equity, I aim to focus on underserved communities, improving access to healthcare and promoting preventative care through education and outreach initiatives.

In addition to my medical ambitions, I am passionate about culinary arts and the role nutrition plays in overall health. I aspire to become a better chef, blending my love for cooking with my commitment to wellness. My long-term vision is to create a platform that bridges health, cooking, and community engagement. This platform would serve as a space where people can access nutritious, culturally relevant recipes, learn about the links between diet and health, and participate in discussions that promote healthy living, particularly in marginalized communities. Ultimately, I hope to empower others to make informed decisions about their health while fostering a sense of community and well-being.

Global Interests

I am deeply passionate about global health initiatives, particularly in understanding how healthcare systems can be improved to better serve underserved and marginalized communities around the world. I believe that access to healthcare is a fundamental right, yet many regions, especially in low- and middle-income countries, face significant barriers to receiving even basic medical care. My interest lies in finding innovative solutions to these challenges, from improving healthcare infrastructure to promoting health education, training local healthcare workers, and enhancing access to life-saving medications and resources. I am committed to contributing to efforts that bridge these healthcare gaps, whether through research, policy advocacy, or hands-on work in global health settings.

In addition to healthcare, I am equally passionate about sustainable food systems and their critical role in promoting both human and environmental health. I believe that the way we grow, produce, and distribute food has far-reaching impacts not only on individual health outcomes but also on the health of our planet. By advocating for sustainable agricultural practices, equitable food distribution, and reducing food waste, I hope to play a role in creating systems that provide nutritious, accessible food to all while minimizing harm to the environment. My long-term vision is to contribute to initiatives that integrate sustainable agriculture with public health, ensuring that communities around the world can thrive with access to healthy, sustainable food sources. Through a multidisciplinary approach that connects healthcare, food systems, and environmental sustainability, I hope to help shape a future where both people and the planet can flourish.

The Casio Calculator Watch and the Evolution of Computation in Everyday Life

The Casio Calculator Watch is an important artifact that highlights the integration of computation into our daily lives. Its introduction created a shift in how individuals interacted with computation tools, as we transitioned from large specialized machines to small personal devices. The calculator watch symbolized the transition of computation from purely educational formats to wearable technology. It increases accessibility to computation and creates a new relationship between humans and computation systems that define today’s world. The watch’s historical significance and its relationships to texts such as Your Computer is on Fire, "When Did the Fire Start", and "Why AI Isn't Going to Make Art" highlight both the potential and perils of computation as it shapes human behavior and thought processes.

As argued in Your Computer is on Fire, computation has been of increasing accessibility (Mullaney 8). The Casio Calculator Watch is an example of this trend as it embedded computation power into an everyday tool, thereby merging utility and effectiveness. By integrating timekeeping and basic arithmetic into a single object, the calculator watch was a step toward the idea of technology seamlessly operating within society. This integration was already conceived in Your Computer is on Fire, where computers are not only tools but necessary parts of society (Mullaney 6). For example, the book discusses the change of computation from standalone machines to almost invisible infrastructures that power smartphones, homes, and AI. Supporting this, the wearable calculator watch exemplifies this as it provides society with a glimpse into the future of computation as pervasive yet indispensable.

In contrast, "When Did the Fire Start" explores the ethical and structural consequences of the integration of computation and technology (Hicks 22). It emphasizes how computation can influence power dynamics and societal structures. Unlike the straightforward use of the calculator watch, AI algorithms operate within unclear frameworks, leading to a few corporations and governments concentrating control over them. The calculator watch, in contrast, empowers individuals directly as it provides them with the ability to perform simple calculations anytime and anywhere. This user-designed object is starkly different from the centralized, and even exploitative nature of modern computation systems. As argued in "When Did the Fire Start", the monopoly of power over data leads to immense control over decision-making processes (Hicks 21). This causes an unsafe power dynamic that emphasizes the evolution from empowering accessibility through the calculator watch to the complex and coercive nature of current-day computation systems.

Similarly, "Why AI Isn't Going to Make Art" shows how technology can shape and potentially undermine human creativity (Chiang). The Casio Calculator Watch only helps us with an easy form of computing as it only helps with replacing mental arithmetic, a basic cognitive task. However, as "Why AI Isn't Going to Make Art" argues, by giving complex tasks, such as art creation and decision-making, to AI, society risks diminishing uniquely human activities (Chiang). The article explains how generative art tools automate creativity and devalue the intentional, reflective processes that define human art. While the calculator watch symbolizes a period where computation was only used to help human ability without fundamentally altering our role, current-day AI systems show how computation can encroach upon our human-specific aspects such as art creation. This raises significant and urgent questions about the future of creativity and critical thought.

Despite these differences, the calculator watch and modern technologies reveal the significant impact of computation on daily life. As Your Computer is on Fire highlights, one of the main goals of computation has been to simplify tasks and integrate them effectively into our daily lives. The calculator watch does this in a very straightforward manner by combining timekeeping and arithmetic into a wearable device. Expanding on this significantly, smartphones merge communication, navigation, and arithmetic into a single portable device, following the same path taken by Casio in its creation of the calculator watch. While enhancing convenience, this centralization of human tasks into a single device reflects the deeper integration between computation and machines, as mentioned in both Your Computer is on Fire and "When Did the Fire Start" (Mullaney 3; Hicks 19). These texts underscore the potential for the integration to cloud how computation systems can exert control, thereby raising concerns regarding transparency, privacy, and user control.

Furthermore, the centralization of power links the calculator watch to modern-day computation systems, as argued in Your Computer is on Fire and "When Did the Fire Start" (Mullaney 7; Hicks 18). The watch empowered individuals by providing them with computational capability. This was an example of democratizing computation power as it increased the number of users who were able to engage with computation for their own purposes. However, modern computation systems, such as AI tools, do the opposite and centralize control in ways that reinforce existing hierarchies. For example, "When Did the Fire Start" argues how corporations and governments use AI to gather and exploit personal data (Hicks 21). This shows how AI tools create a system that prioritizes profit and control over user empowerment, unlike the calculator watch. This shift from user-centric design to centralizing control exemplifies the tension in the development of current-day computation systems.

The legacy of the Casio Calculator Watch provides a valuable perspective on the progression of computation from a simple tool to a deeply integrated and influential force in society. As Your Computer is on Fire illustrates, the calculator watch exemplified the early promise of technology to integrate seamlessly into daily life while enhancing human capabilities (Mullaney 6). At the same time, "When Did the Fire Start" and "Why AI Isn't Going to Make Art" highlight the need to scrutinize the broader implications of this integration, particularly as computational systems grow more opaque and influential (Hicks 23; Chiang). By examining the watch in the context of these texts, we can better understand how computation has shaped human life, from its empowering beginnings to its more complex and criticized present.

Therefore, the Casio Calculator Watch provides a unique lens for understanding the historical and ongoing relationship between computation and society. Its role as an early example of accessible, user-centric technology reflects the potential of computation to enhance human abilities. However, as Your Computer is on Fire, "When Did the Fire Start", and "Why AI Isn't Going to Make Art" argue, the evolution of computational systems raises important questions about control and the societal impacts of technological dependence (Mullaney 6; Hicks 20; Chiang). By discussing these important topics, we can better navigate the promises and perils of a world increasingly shaped by computation, ensuring that technology serves to enrich, rather than diminish, our human control.
